# Content Playbook

Extra reading for the Daily Content Spark skill. Open this when you need a hook formula, a post shape, platform rules, or a fix for a flat draft.

## 1. Pick the platform

| Platform | Limit | Aim for | Hashtags | What the reader wants |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | 3000 chars | 120–220 words | 3–5 | one useful lesson from real work or life |
| X | 280 chars | 40–60 words | 0–2 | one sharp thought they can repost |
| Instagram | 2200 chars | 80–150 words | 3–8 | a story that matches the image |
| Threads | 500 chars | 60–100 words | 0–3 | a casual thought, like a text to a friend |
| Newsletter | 5000 chars | 300–600 words | 0–3 | one story, one takeaway, one next step |

Platform notes:

- **LinkedIn:** the first two lines show before "see more". Make line one work alone. Use short paragraphs and blank lines.
- **X:** no wind-up. One idea. If you need a thread, the first post must still make sense alone.
- **Instagram:** the caption supports the image; it does not describe it. Put the hook in the first line before the caption is cut off.
- **Threads:** conversational. Lower the polish. Questions work well.
- **Newsletter:** write to one person. Use a subject line under 50 characters, a short opening scene, and one clear next step.

Never stretch a small thought to fill a big limit. A 90-word LinkedIn post beats a padded 250-word one.

## 2. Find the one point

Before drafting, finish this sentence:

> The one thing I want [audience] to remember is ______.

If it takes two sentences, you have two posts. Pick the one with a real detail behind it and save the other.

Ask the user for the missing piece instead of making it up:

- Where were you?
- What did you do, in one concrete action?
- What changed after that?
- What did you expect, and what happened instead?

## 3. Hook formulas

Use these as starting points, then rewrite in the user's voice. Never use a formula that needs a fact the user did not give you.

1. **Specific result** — "I deleted four apps. I got two hours back."
   Only use when the user gave the real number.
2. **Contradiction** — "My quietest week was the week I got the most done."
   The two halves must both be true.
3. **Small scene** — "Sunday, 7:40 a.m. Laptop open before the curtains."
   Concrete time and place beat a summary.
4. **Direct address** — "If you open one app before you decide to, read this."
   Name the reader's exact behaviour, not a vague group.
5. **Tension question** — "What would you do with two extra hours a week?"
   Ask a question the post actually answers.
6. **Confession** — "I was wrong about what makes a walk useful."
   Must be a real change of mind, not a fake weakness.
7. **Real list** — "Four apps. Two hours. One rule."
   Short fragments work only when the numbers are real.

Avoid hooks that start with: "In this post", "Let me tell you", "I want to talk about", "Here is a quick thought". They delay the point.

## 4. Post shapes

**Lesson**
1. Hook
2. What happened (2–3 sentences, real detail)
3. What you thought would happen
4. What you learned
5. CTA

**Story**
1. Hook
2. Scene: time, place, one action
3. Turn: the surprise or mistake
4. Takeaway in one sentence
5. CTA

**List**
1. Hook with the number of items (only if the number is real)
2. One line of context
3. Three items, each: the action + why it helped
4. CTA

**Before / After**
1. Hook
2. Before: the old habit, stated plainly
3. What changed, in one action
4. After: the real difference
5. CTA

**Contrarian** (only when the user actually disagrees)
1. Hook: the common advice
2. Why it fails in your experience
3. What to do instead
4. CTA

## 5. Voice rules

- Write like the user talks. Short sentences. One idea per sentence.
- Use concrete nouns: "four apps", "a 20-minute walk", not "digital wellness" or "intentional living".
- Active voice: "I deleted the apps", not "the apps were deleted".
- Say "I" when it is the user's experience. Do not hide behind "we" or "one".
- Keep one rough, human line. Perfect symmetry sounds generated.
- Match any writing sample the user provides. Their rhythm beats these rules.
- No fake vulnerability, no invented struggle, no "I used to be terrible at everything".
- Do not add a moral to every sentence. One takeaway is enough.

## 6. Cut list

Cut unless removing the word changes the meaning:

`very`, `really`, `just`, `actually`, `basically`, `literally`, `simply`, `quite`, `in order to`, `at the end of the day`, `it is important to note`, `needless to say`, `a lot of`, `kind of`, `sort of`.

Replace these clichés with the plain thing:

| Cliché | Say instead |
|---|---|
| in today's fast-paced world | (delete it) |
| game-changer | say what changed |
| dive into | start, read, try |
| unlock | get, open, find |
| leverage | use |
| delve | look at |
| tapestry | mix, mess, pattern |
| testament to | shows |
| elevate | improve, raise |
| transformative | say what changed |
| revolutionize | change |

Also cut:

- The first sentence if it only clears your throat.
- The last sentence if it repeats the takeaway.
- Any sentence that sounds like a slogan.
- Extra hashtags no one would search for.

## 7. CTA bank

Pick one. A post with two asks gets neither.

**Start a conversation**
- What is one small thing you would change this week? Tell me below.
- Which part of this have you seen happen?
- Disagree? Reply with the strongest counter-argument.
- What would you add to this list?

**Get saves and shares**
- If this helped, save it and send it to one person who needs it.
- Keep this for the next time you [situation].
- Share it with someone who is in the middle of this.

**Build a habit or follow-up**
- I am testing this for 7 days. Want the follow-up? Comment "day 1".
- I will post what happens next week. Follow if you want the result.
- Try it once and reply with what you noticed.

Do not use "Comment YES if...", "Tag a friend", or "Like if you agree". They read as bait and reduce trust.

## 8. Rewrites: bad to good

**Bad**
> In today's fast-paced world, it is important to note that we are all very busy. I recently decided to leverage a game-changer: deleting apps. It was truly transformative and unlocked a lot of time.

Why it fails: wind-up hook, three clichés, no real detail, no point.

**Good**
> I deleted four apps last week.
>
> Not because they are bad. Because I opened them before I decided to.
>
> The quiet part: I did not miss any of them. [add what you did with the time]
>
> If one app opens before you decide, delete it for 7 days. Then tell me what you noticed.

**Bad**
> I wanted to share some thoughts on productivity. There are many ways to be more productive, but one thing that really helped me was walking. Walking is a great way to clear your head and come up with new ideas.

Why it fails: no scene, no specifics, tells the reader what to think.

**Good**
> My best ideas do not arrive at a desk.
>
> They arrive 20 minutes into a walk with no headphones.
>
> Boredom is not wasted time. It is where the next idea finds you.

**Bad**
> I am excited to announce that I have completed a new project. It was a challenging journey but I learned a lot. Grateful for the support.

Why it fails: announcement with no story, vague "challenging journey".

**Good**
> I shipped the thing.
>
> It took three weekends, two rewrites, and one argument with the CSS.
>
> The part I did not expect: [add the real surprise]
>
> What is the smallest thing you shipped this month?

## 9. Fact and privacy check

Before the user posts:

- Every number, name, date, and quote comes from the user or a source they can link.
- Quotes from other people need their permission, even if the quote is nice.
- Do not share a client's, employer's, or family member's private story without permission.
- Do not invent a result, testimonial, or "someone told me" story.
- No medical, legal, or financial claims unless the user is qualified and has evidence.
- If a detail is missing, write `[add the real detail]`. Do not fill the gap with something plausible.
- Ask: would the user be comfortable if the person in the story read this?

## 10. 60-second edit

1. Read it out loud. Fix anything you stumble on.
2. Check the first line. Does it make sense with no context?
3. Find the one point. Delete anything that does not support it.
4. Cut one adjective from every sentence. Cut one sentence from every paragraph.
5. Confirm there is exactly one CTA.
6. Check names, numbers, and links.
7. Run `python scripts/check_draft.py draft.md --platform <name>` and fix every `FIX`.
